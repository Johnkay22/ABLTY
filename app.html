RV CALIBRATION
How this is calculated
RV Calibration compares your recent RV performance to your earlier baseline to show whether your signal is improving, stable, or dropping.
FORMULA
1) Your RV sessions are ordered oldest to newest.
2) They are split into two halves: early sessions and recent sessions.
3) Early average and recent average are compared.
4) The displayed calibration score is your recent average.
5) The trend arrow and delta show how much recent sessions changed vs early sessions.
PSI ANALYTICS
How to read your stats
What is a p-value?
It measures how likely your results are if you were just guessing randomly. Lower = stronger evidence that something beyond chance is happening.
p < 0.01 Strong evidence — less than 1% chance it's random
p < 0.05 Statistically significant — less than 5% chance
p < 0.20 Suggestive trend — not yet significant
p > 0.20 Could be chance — keep training
HOW EACH PROTOCOL IS SCORED
Zener — Hit rate compared to 20% chance (5 symbols, each equally likely).

Presentiment — Your tap-to-event timing gap is compared against 10,000 randomized shuffles. If your timing is closer than most random pairings, the p-value drops.

REALITY CHECK
Perceptual training for lucid dreaming
01 / 05 - How It Works
Lucid Dream Training
To have a lucid dream you need to recognize you're dreaming while it's happening. The obstacle: your dreaming mind accepts everything as real by default.

Reality checks train the habit of deliberate awareness. Do them consistently while awake and your sleeping mind eventually asks the same question — and catches the answer.

Based on LaBerge (1980, Stanford) and Stumbrys et al. (2012) systematic review of lucid dream induction techniques.
02 / 05 - The Pager
Random Triggers
Checks are sent at random intervals throughout your waking hours — not on a fixed schedule. Randomness is key. If you knew when to expect them, you'd anticipate them instead of genuinely checking.

Research supports 5–10 checks per day as the effective range. Each check takes under 30 seconds, but the mental pause is the point — not the tap.
03 / 05 - Physical Checks
Physical Checks
Nose pinch: Pinch your nose shut and try to breathe through it. In waking life: impossible. In a dream: you can still breathe. Binary and unmistakable once trained.

Hand check: Look at your hands. Count your fingers slowly. In dreams, hands are frequently malformed — extra fingers, fused digits, shifting shapes.

Text check: Read something near you. Look away, then read it again. In dreams, text almost always changes or becomes unreadable the second time.
04 / 05 - Mental Checks
Location & Strangeness
The critical question: Don't just perform the check mechanically. Genuinely ask yourself — 'Could this actually be a dream right now?' Pause with it. Visualize what becoming lucid would feel like. This is the step most people skip, and it's the most important one.

Location memory: Dreams rarely have coherent origin stories. Ask yourself how you got here. If you can't trace it, that's a signal.

Strangeness rating: Rate your environment 1–5 for anything that feels subtly off. Training this perception while awake is what carries it into dreams.
05 / 05 - The WBTB Window
The WBTB Window
The most effective intervention isn't the daytime checks — it's what happens after your first 5–6 hours of sleep.

When the WBTB alarm wakes you, stay awake for 20–60 minutes and practice the MILD technique: recall your last dream, identify a strange element in it, visualize yourself back in that dream becoming lucid, and repeat the intention 'Next time I'm dreaming, I will recognize I'm dreaming.' Then return to sleep.

Research shows WBTB + MILD produces lucid dreams in up to 54% of participants. The daytime checks build the habit — this window is the mechanism.
